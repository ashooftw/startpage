Section 01
Mindset & Foundation

Install the right mental operating system before writing a single command. Most people who fail aren't stopped by content — they're stopped by their own head. This section is not optional.

Brutal honesty upfront: Cybersecurity is genuinely hard. It rewards obsessive curiosity. If you don't find yourself wanting to go down rabbit holes at 2am, this will feel like a grind. Get honest with yourself about this in week one.
🧠
Core Mental Frameworks
  • ▸Growth mindset + ownership: Every failed CTF or broken lab is data, not a verdict on your intelligence. Treat it like debugging a puzzle.
  • ▸Ethics above everything: Permission always. These skills can ruin lives or land you in prison. Only test what you're explicitly authorized to test. Non-negotiable.
  • ▸Consistency beats intensity: 8 focused hours > 12 scattered hours. Use Pomodoro (50/10). Track everything in a daily journal.
  • ▸Imposter syndrome is normal: Everyone feels it. The cure is shipping projects and publishing writeups.
  • ▸Think like an attacker: Every system was built by humans. Humans make mistakes. Your job is to find them before the bad guys do.
⏱
Daily Habit Stack
8–10 hours, structured
  • ▸Morning (3 hrs): Video + reading. Absorb new theory with zero distractions.
  • ▸Afternoon (4 hrs): Hands-on labs. TryHackMe, HackTheBox, home lab. Do → fail → fix → repeat.
  • ▸Evening (2 hrs): Write up what you learned. Document. Update GitHub or blog.
  • ▸Wind-down (1 hr): Light reading. Krebs on Security, The Hacker News, r/netsec.
  • ▸Every day: One small win. Finish one room, one lesson, one writeup section.

Section 02
Core Technical Skills

These are prerequisites. The soil everything else grows in. Skip them and you'll waste months later hitting walls you could have prevented. 6–8 weeks of intense focus, then revisit continuously.

Timeline: Months 1–3 are heavy on these foundations while you start light cyber rooms in parallel. Goal: by month 3, you can navigate Linux, subnet a network on paper, and write a 50-line Python script from scratch.
Priority 1 — Most Critical
Networking Fundamentals
Everything in cybersecurity is networking. Without this, you're essentially blind.
  • ▸OSI model and TCP/IP stack — know every layer and what can go wrong at each one
  • ▸IP addressing, subnetting, CIDR notation — practice until you can subnet in your head
  • ▸Core protocols: DNS, DHCP, HTTP/S, FTP, SSH, SMTP, ARP — how each works AND how each can be attacked
  • ▸Firewalls, routers, switches, VLANs, NAT — understand what controls traffic and why
  • ▸Packet analysis with Wireshark — start this early, it's pre-installed in Kali

  • ▸TryHackMe: Pre-Security + Jr Penetration Tester paths

Section 04
Certifications Roadmap

Theory certs get you past HR filters. Practical certs prove you can actually do the work. Get them in order. Study for free — pay only when you're ready to sit the exam.

Golden rule: Never pay for video courses when free, high-quality alternatives exist (Messer, PortSwigger, TCM YouTube). Spend your money on exam vouchers, not content.
01
CompTIA Security+ (SY0-701)
Start First Month 3–5 ~$400 USD exam
The industry-standard entry cert. Required or preferred by a large majority of SOC Analyst and IT Security roles. Validates broad foundational knowledge. Widely recognized by non-technical HR staff in India and globally. Study entirely free with Professor Messer's complete video course + practice tests. Supplement with Jason Dion's Udemy course when on sale ($15).
Free: Professor Messer YouTubeFree: Messer's study notesFree quizzes: examcompass.com
Difficulty ★★☆☆☆ · Study time: 6–8 weeks full-time
02
eJPT — eLearnSecurity Junior Penetration Tester
Offensive Track Month 6–7 ~$200 USD
100% hands-on exam. No multiple choice. You get a network to attack and must find flags by actually exploiting systems. Excellent confidence builder for beginners. INE provides free learning content to prepare. Strong recognition in the Indian market, especially at MSSPs and mid-size companies. Far better value than CEH for a beginner offensive track.
Difficulty ★★★☆☆ · Study time: 6–8 weeks of solid labs
02b
CompTIA CySA+ — Blue Team Alternative
Defensive Track Month 6–8 ~$370 USD
If your target is SOC Analyst or threat detection roles rather than pentesting, take CySA+ instead of eJPT. Covers threat and vulnerability management, SIEM operations, incident response, and software security. Very relevant to the SOC L1 → L2 career path. Pairs perfectly with Security+.
Difficulty ★★★☆☆ · Study time: 8–10 weeks
03
PNPT — Practical Network Penetration Tester (TCM Security)
Offensive Track Month 9–10 ~$399 USD
Realistic, scenario-based practical exam. You conduct a full pentest and write a professional report. Created by TCM Security — a highly respected name in the community. Strong recognition with smaller companies and consultancies. Much closer to real-world pentesting than CEH. Great stepping stone before OSCP.
Difficulty ★★★★☆ · Study time: TCM PEH course + 3 months of labs
04
OSCP — Offensive Security Certified Professional
Gold Standard Month 14–18 ~$1,499 USD
The most respected offensive security certification in the world. 24-hour hands-on practical exam — zero multiple choice, zero theory. You get a network of machines and must root as many as possible, then write a professional report. Having OSCP on your resume is a complete career game-changer. Plan for $1,499 (includes PEN-200 course, 90-day lab access, and 1 exam attempt). Many candidates need 2 attempts. DO NOT rush this — prepare for 3–6 months minimum before attempting.
Prerequisites for OSCP: Linux solid, Python scripting comfortable, 30+ HTB machines (including mediums), AD attack basics, manual exploitation (no Metasploit dependency). If you're not there yet, wait.
Difficulty ★★★★★ · Study time: 3–6 months dedicated prep

Section 07
Freelancing & Early Income

Realistic about timelines. You can start earning in months 6–8 — well before landing a full-time job. Treat early income as skill-building and portfolio evidence first, cash second.

Legal non-negotiable: Only test systems you own or have explicit written authorization to test. Bug bounty programs give you this permission formally. Unauthorized testing is a criminal offense under India's IT Act 2000 (Section 66) and can result in 3–7 years imprisonment. No exceptions.

Section 08
Job Hunting Strategy

The goal isn't the perfect job. It's the first real job that gets you real-world experience. Be strategic, not proud. Rejection is part of the process — volume matters.

India context: Degree preference exists at large enterprises (TCS, Wipro, Infosys) for fresh roles. But startups, MSSPs, product companies, consulting firms, and remote-first global companies care about skills + certs + portfolio. Target those first.
Target Roles (First Job)
SOC Analyst (Tier 1)
Easiest Entry
₹3.5–6L/yr · High volume of openings
Monitor alerts, triage incidents, escalate to L2. Security+ usually enough. MSSPs, banks, and large corporates hire heavily. Great real-world experience even if not glamorous. Clear path to L2/L3 and then specialization.
Junior Penetration Tester
Higher Reward
₹5–10L/yr · More competitive
Higher technical bar. Need eJPT or PNPT + solid CTF history + portfolio. More competitive but significantly better salary growth trajectory. Boutique security firms and MSSPs are your target, not the big 4 IT companies.
Vulnerability Analyst
₹4–7L/yr · Good entry point
Run vulnerability scans, analyze results, prioritize and communicate risk. Security+ + scanning tool knowledge sufficient. Good bridge between SOC and pentesting roles.
IT Security Analyst / GRC Analyst
₹3–6L/yr · Commerce background advantage
Your commerce background actually helps here. Governance, risk, compliance work involves business process understanding. ISO 27001, GDPR, PCI-DSS context. Bridge role with a path toward CISO track.

Section 10
Long-Term Growth

First job is just the starting line. Here's how to engineer your trajectory from junior to elite — intentionally, over 5–10 years.

Years 1–3
First Job → Senior Professional
Target: ₹8–18L/yr · Senior Pentester / SOC L2–L3 / Security Engineer
  • ▸Say yes to everything in your first role. Do the boring work. Learn how real enterprise security operates — it's different from CTFs.
  • ▸Find a mentor inside or outside the company. This is worth more than any certification.
  • ▸Continue HTB and bug bounty work outside working hours — keep the edge sharp.
  • ▸OSCP if not done yet (Year 1). Then look toward OSEP (advanced exploitation) or CRTP (Active Directory) depending on your path.
  • ▸Blue team path: GREM, GCIH, or CySA+ → Senior SOC → Detection Engineering lead.
  • ▸Build internal tools, automate repetitive tasks, write documentation — this is how you become indispensable.
Years 3–7
Mid-Level → Specialist → Public Reputation
Target: ₹18–40L/yr India · $80–130K remote · Principal / Lead / Consultant
  • ▸Specialize deeply: Cloud Security (AWS/GCP/Azure), AppSec, IoT Security, Red Team, Forensics, Threat Intelligence — pick one and own it.
  • ▸Submit CFP proposals to security conferences: null meets (India), OWASP chapter talks, c0c0n Kerala, ClubHack, BSides India — start small and build up.
  • ▸Publish security research: a CVE, a blog series on a novel technique, a tool that others find useful — this is how you become known beyond your employer.
  • ▸Build a public reputation: speak, write, contribute to open-source security tools — GitHub stars, Twitter/X followers, and LinkedIn presence compound dramatically at this stage.
  • ▸Consider independent consulting: charge ₹30,000–₹1,00,000/day for specialized assessments once you have the track record.
Years 7–15
Elite Track — Recognized Expert
Target: ₹50L–₹1.5Cr+ India · $150K–$400K+ global remote · Own firm possible
  • ▸CISO track: Add an MBA or executive education. Business skills become paramount. ₹50L–₹1Cr+/yr territory.
  • ▸Independent consultant: Boutique firm or solo practice. Specialized expertise + reputation = premium rates.
  • ▸Bug bounty elite: Top 100 HackerOne researchers earn $100K–$500K/yr. This takes 5–8 years of serious effort.
  • ▸Security researcher: CVEs, Defcon/Blackhat talks, tool contributions, academic collaboration. Your name becomes your brand.
  • ▸Continuous learning is non-negotiable at every stage: Read technical blogs, follow researchers, attend virtual events. The moment you stop learning, you start falling behind.
The hidden advantage you already have: You're starting in 2025–26 in India, which has one of the fastest-growing cybersecurity job markets in the world. The demand-supply gap for skilled professionals is enormous. A self-taught, well-documented, cert-holding security professional with a public portfolio in this market is not disadvantaged — they're rare.
The Final Truth
